## Day 66 — The Hidden Cliff of Pride

> *The victory over one passion is not the end of the journey, but the beginning of a deeper humility.*

You may think the greatest dangers lie at the beginning of the spiritual life, when desire still pulls strongly toward Egypt. Yet there is another danger that often waits farther along the road. Once the poison of obvious sins begins to lose its grip, a quieter temptation appears. The heart begins to admire its own progress. What lust could not accomplish, pride now attempts.

Notice how swiftly the people exchanged one passion for another. Having been healed from the serpents, they imagined themselves worthy of honors God had not given them. They mistook spiritual progress for spiritual superiority. This is the subtle deception of the false self: it is willing even to use virtue as a ladder for self-exaltation.

Do not measure your growth by how much better you believe yourself to be than others. The closer you draw to God, the less interested you become in comparing yourself at all. Christ, who possessed every right to glory, chose instead the path of humility, washing feet and bearing a cross. If the Holy One descended in love, why should we seek to ascend by our own importance?

Every passion, whatever its disguise, pulls the soul downward. But humility always lifts us because it places us where grace can find us. Therefore, when you notice pride quietly taking root beneath your victories, do not despair. Simply bow your heart again before Christ. The safest place on the mountain is not the highest ledge, but the place where you still know your need of God.

**Breath Prayer**

> **Inhale:** *Keep me humble, Lord.*

> **Exhale:** *Let grace be enough.*
