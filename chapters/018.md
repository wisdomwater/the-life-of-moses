## Day 18 — The Cost of Freedom

> *Do not mistake greater resistance for a lack of God's presence.*

Once Moses had been prepared by silence, light, and long obedience, he was finally ready to announce freedom. The same man whose words had once been dismissed now spoke with a quiet authority born not from self-confidence but from communion with God. Before we seek to guide others, we must first allow God to shape us. The most enduring words are those that have been forged in the wilderness.

Yet the promise of freedom did not immediately make life easier. Pharaoh answered Moses' message by increasing the burden of the people. This should not surprise you. Whenever the soul begins to loosen its chains, the old tyrant resists. Temptations that once seemed asleep awaken, fears become louder, and old habits fight to reclaim their place.

Do not conclude that you have taken the wrong path simply because the struggle has intensified. Often the fiercest opposition comes not before you begin to change, but after you have already turned toward Christ. The enemy is content with a soul that remains asleep, but he is troubled by one that has begun to awaken. What feels like greater darkness may simply be the shadow cast by a brighter light.

The Lord does not abandon His work because your courage falters. Like Israel, you may wonder whether freedom is worth the hardship it demands. Yet Christ continues to lead you forward with patient love, strengthening what is weak until your fear is transformed into trust. Keep walking. The wilderness is not proof that God has forgotten you—it is often the very road by which He teaches you to live as one who is truly free.

The tyrant would gladly keep you making bricks from clay forever, endlessly filling desires that can never remain full. But Christ calls you to build something altogether different: a life founded not on passing pleasures but on the enduring joy of communion with God. Every step away from the clay is another step toward the kingdom that cannot be shaken.
