## Day 61 — Beyond the Reach of Envy

> *The soul clothed in Christ need not answer every wound with another wound.*

Envy is a strange sorrow. It does not grieve because something has been lost, but because another person has received something good. It turns a neighbor’s joy into a personal injury and finds secret comfort in another’s misfortune. Like a fire shut inside the heart, it consumes the one who carries it long before it harms the person against whom it burns.

Even Moses was struck at by envy. Aaron and Miriam, those nearest to him, turned their words against him. Yet their arrows could not reach the place where Moses had learned to dwell. He had stood upon the Rock, sheltered beneath the hand of God, and followed where the Lord led. His security no longer depended upon the praise or understanding of others.

Moses did not defend himself by returning accusation for accusation. When judgment fell upon those who had wounded him, he prayed for their healing. This is one of the clearest signs that he had truly followed God: he desired mercy for those who had caused him pain. Their envy did not become his bitterness.

Christ reveals this path completely. He did not answer hatred with hatred, but entrusted Himself to the Father and prayed for those who harmed Him. To put on Christ is to become protected, not from every wound, but from the need to pass the wound onward. His love becomes an armor through which resentment cannot easily pierce.

When envy or accusation comes near you, do not let it draw you down into the same sickness. Remain upon the Rock. Let Christ guard the tender places within you, and ask for the grace to desire healing even for those who misunderstand you. In this way, the arrow may strike, but it will not determine who you become.

**Breath Prayer**

> Inhale: *Clothe me in Christ.*

> Exhale: *Teach me to answer with mercy.*
